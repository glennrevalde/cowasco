# G-SMaRT Integrated Cooperative System

Formerly G-SMaRT Accounting. Version 27 makes it a multipurpose cooperative system: one Party Master for every person or organization, whatever roles it holds (member, customer, supplier, employee), lines of business with member and nonmember rules, the names of the Party Master on every document, reporting by line of business with inter-unit eliminations, and the Consolidated Party Statement. See § 5, `UPGRADE_v27.txt` and `docs/MULTIPURPOSE_IMPLEMENTATION.md`.

A web-based accounting system in plain PHP and MySQL. It covers the general ledger (the Financial menu: chart of accounts, journal entries and financial statements), Fixed Assets, Customers (A/R), Vendors (A/P), Inventory, Point of Sale, and Banking. It also includes System Administration: user accounts, user role configuration, page access configuration, and a permanent audit trail.

It also includes a **Cooperative Management System** for Philippine cooperatives: members, share capital, savings and time deposits, loans, teller cashiering, interest on share capital and patronage refund, and CDA compliance, all posting to the same general ledger. See § 5 and `docs/COOP_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md`.

**Version 28 adds the Water Billing module**: the former stand-alone water-billing program (meter reading, tariffs, rate codes, billing periods, bills and statements, one-time / installment / recurring bills, discounts, collections and official receipts, corrections, disconnection, water reports) now runs inside this system with one login, one menu, one Party Master, one inventory ledger and one general ledger. Water-service accounts belong to parties (a water customer is not made a member); every water bill, receipt, correction and material issuance posts once to the general ledger under the Water Utility line of business; the former water accounting, inventory and user pages are retired in favour of the cooperative ones. The former water database is brought in with `tools/create_target.php` + `tools/migrate_water.php` (dry run, idempotent, rollback). Start with `docs/DELIVERY_REPORT.md`; installation and cut-over: `docs/DEPLOYMENT_XAMPP.md`.

There is no framework and no build step. It runs on XAMPP, WAMP, Laragon, or any PHP host.

## 1. Requirements

| | |
|---|---|
| PHP | 8.1 or newer (8.2+ recommended), with the `pdo_mysql` and `mbstring` extensions (both are on by default in XAMPP) |
| Database | MySQL 5.5.3 or newer (5.5, 5.6, 5.7, 8.x) or MariaDB 5.5 or newer |
| Web server | Apache (the `.htaccess` files are included) or nginx (see section 9) |
| Browser | Any current browser. Everything it loads (Bootstrap, icons, the date picker, fonts) is in the program folder; nothing comes from the internet. |

**PHP and MySQL versions are separate.** The database can be as old as MySQL 5.5, but the PHP code needs PHP 8.1 or newer. Old XAMPP packages that include MySQL 5.5 (XAMPP 1.7.4 to 1.8.2) come with PHP 5, which will not run this system. With those, install a current XAMPP, or point a PHP 8 install at your existing MySQL 5.5 server in `config/config.php`.

## 2. Install with XAMPP and phpMyAdmin (easiest)

1. Unzip the folder into `C:\xampp\htdocs\` so you have `C:\xampp\htdocs\accounting-system\index.php`.
2. Start **Apache** and **MySQL** in the XAMPP Control Panel.
3. Open <http://localhost/phpmyadmin>, go to **Import**, choose one of the files below, and click **Go**:
   - `database/accounting_system.sql`: the complete database **with sample data**, about two and a half months of transactions in every module, a sample cooperative (two branches, 12 individual members, a joint account and an institution, savings, time deposits and loans) and a **sample water utility** (see "Sample water utility" in § 4).
   - `database/accounting_system_clean.sql`: the same structure and setup (chart of accounts, tax codes, terms, roles, page access, users, sites, POS terminals, the cooperative's set-up lists, and the water billing set-up: water accounts in the chart and their module defaults, rate codes and tariff tiers, pipe sizes, the Senior Citizen and Employee discounts, eight bill codes, the water stockroom and its materials) plus a few sample customers, vendors, and items, with **no transactions**, no cooperative member or product and no water-service account. Use this one to start real bookkeeping. Edit or deactivate the sample names first; set up the cooperative as in `docs/COOP_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md` § 4, and review the water tariff, discounts and bill codes (Water Billing > Rates & Discounts, Bill Codes) before the first bill.

   To bring in the former water program's export files (Account Master and Billing Master), start from `accounting_system_clean.sql` and use Water Billing > Setup > Data Import Management: see `docs/WATER_IMPORT_FILES.md`.

   Either file creates the database `cooperative_system` (the name in `config/config.php`) with all 229 tables (146 of the cooperative system, 83 of water billing) and the two views the water pages read; nothing else needs to be imported. Neither is for bringing in a former water database: for that, install with `php tools/install.php` (no water set-up) and follow `docs/DEPLOYMENT_XAMPP.md` § 3, since the migration brings the former rates, bill codes and accounts itself.
4. If your MySQL `root` user has a password, or you used a different database name, edit `config/config.php`.
5. Open <http://localhost/accounting-system/> and log in with **admin / Admin@12345**. You will be asked to choose a new password right away.

## 3. Install from the command line (alternative)

```bash
php tools/install.php                  # creates the database, tables, triggers and setup data
php tools/install.php --demo           # same, plus sample transactions
php tools/install.php --water-starter  # adds the water billing set-up (what accounting_system_clean.sql has)
php tools/install.php --water-demo     # --demo + --water-starter + the sample water utility (what accounting_system.sql has)
php tools/install.php --drop           # drops and recreates the database first (DELETES ALL DATA)
php tools/export_sql.php --out=database/my_copy.sql   # writes the current database as one importable file
```

Database settings come from `config/config.php`. You can override them with the environment variables `DB_HOST`, `DB_PORT`, `DB_NAME`, `DB_USER`, and `DB_PASS`. With `--demo` and `--water-demo`, sample dates are generated relative to the day you run the installer. `--water-demo` makes the water documents through the water pages themselves (it starts PHP's built-in web server for a minute, or uses `php tools/water_demo.php --base=http://localhost/...`).

## 5. What's inside (353 pages)

- **Chart of Accounts:** account list with sub-accounts, journal entries (including opening balances), module default accounts, terms and tax codes. The **Class List** holds classes you name yourself (branch, department, product line, project) and **Employees & Other Names** holds the people who are neither customers nor suppliers. Reports: trial balance, balance sheet, income statement, cash flows, general ledger, journal, **Income Statement by Class**, **Balance Sheet by Class**, **Trial Balance by Date Range**, **Trial Balance of Totals** (the debits and the credits of each account for the period, not the balance) and **Balance Sheet by Date Range**, **Subsidiary Ledger** and **Schedule of Accounts**. An account can be marked **With SL** so it keeps a listing of the names that transacted it. Choosing a name on the Subsidiary Ledger and leaving the account blank lists every account marked With SL for that name, including the ones it has no entries on in the period, so the whole of a supplier's or customer's subsidiary ledger is seen at once. The **Memorized Transaction List** keeps any transaction you use again and again and enters it on a schedule, automatically or after a reminder on the Home page. **Set Up Budgets** holds twelve monthly figures per account for the income statement or the balance sheet, and the two budget reports put the actual figures beside them. Reports can show columns by month, quarter, or year, and every financial report has a Class filter. The journal entry print-out shows Account, Description, Name, Class, Debit and Credit with totals, and Prepared by / Checked by / Approved by lines. On journal entries and cash vouchers the account and name boxes search as you type (by account description or by name), and the class of the entry fills the class of each line.
- **Classes on every transaction:** each transaction form (invoices, sales receipts, payments, bills, checks, cash vouchers, deposits, transfers, item receipts, inventory adjustments and transfers, builds, processing orders, depreciation, POS sales) has a Class box. The class is copied to every ledger line the transaction posts; journal entries and cash vouchers can also set a class per line. A POS terminal can have a default class. When a transaction is saved with no class chosen, the system asks for confirmation first (Choose a class / Continue saving).
- **Fixed Assets:** asset register (with a Location list and a default class per asset; the register, the asset reports and Run Depreciation can be filtered by location), categories, and a monthly depreciation run. When no class is chosen on the run, each asset is depreciated under its own default class. Methods: straight-line, declining balance, sum-of-the-years'-digits, and units of production. Supports opening accumulated depreciation, changes in estimate, and disposal with gain/loss. Reports: register, schedule, by category, additions and disposals.
- **Asset maintenance:** maintenance records per asset with the supplier, spare parts (part no., serial no., model no., size, brand, warranty), labor (man-hours or a bulk charge), the maintenance description, and pictures of the parts. The cost is disbursed through accounting: saving a record enters a Bill, writes a Check Voucher or makes a Cash Voucher, and keeps it in step when the record is edited or voided. **Preventive maintenance** tasks fall due by date and / or by meter (vehicle registration renewal, change oil, cleaning of air-conditioning units, computers and solar systems, anything else). A due task reminds through the bell in the top bar, the Home page and a pop-up after log-in, and keeps reminding until a user acknowledges that it is complete; a repeating task then moves to its next due date. Reports: maintenance history, cost by asset, spare parts used, labor, upcoming preventive maintenance, completed preventive maintenance.
- **Customers:** estimates → sales orders → invoices (partial fulfilment), batch invoices, sales receipts, statement charges, statements, finance charges, payments (early-payment discounts and the taxes the customer withheld, ticked by type: 1% / 2% to Creditable Withholding Tax, 5% VAT to Creditable VAT Withheld; the amount applied can never be more than the amount received, and available credits can be applied to open invoices on the same screen), credit memos and refunds, billable costs, and pending invoices. **Set Up Forecast** holds a sales forecast per customer (or all customers in one sheet) for the year, and **Forecast vs Actual by Customer** shows how the year is going against it. The **Customer Center** narrows its list of customers as you type. Also includes A/R aging, collections, average days to pay, and 20+ sales reports, among them **Income by Customer Summary** and **Income by Customer Detail** (the sales of the items sold to each customer less what they cost: the income or loss, with the margin) and the **Sales Report Through Inventory Movement** (the quantity sold worked out as beginning inventory + deliveries - withdrawals - ending inventory, at the average price of the invoices, batch invoices, sales receipts and POS sales, by site, class and cashier).
- **Vendors:** purchase orders → item receipts → bills (an item receipt not billed yet can be **edited**: date, slip number, site, memo and lines, with the purchase order following), pay bills (by check or card, with the **taxes withheld** from the supplier in a Withholding column: one or more types of the Withholding Tax Table ticked per bill, each worked out on the open amount net of VAT and editable; a bill payment can be **edited** afterwards: its date, account, check number, the bills it pays, the amounts and the taxes withheld, with the bills' balances worked out again), **advances to suppliers** (money paid before the goods or services come, posted to Advances to Suppliers and applied to the supplier's bills in Pay Bills, with an Advances to Suppliers report), each supplier's usual withholding taxes on the vendor record, **supplier debit memos** (returns and billing corrections, applied to the supplier's outstanding bills on their own screen or in Pay Bills), bill tracker, and receipt management (upload photos, PDFs, or e-mails; details are read automatically; then match or create a bill). The **Vendor Center** narrows its list of suppliers as you type. Also includes A/P aging and vendor reports, among them **Expenses by Vendor Summary** (by month, quarter or year if wanted) and **Expenses by Vendor Detail** (every expense charged to each supplier, with its account and class).
- **Inventory:** items (inventory, non-inventory, service, assembly, group, discount, subtotal, payment, and other charge), multiple sites, transfers, quantity/value adjustments, assembly builds (including pending builds; transfers, adjustments and builds can be **edited** after they are saved and are posted again, with the reason in the audit trail), physical counts, price changes, a **Category List** (add, rename and delete item categories; a category still in use is deleted only after its items are moved to another category or to none, so two categories can be merged), and ten price levels per item, each with a name of your own (**Rename price levels** on Set Item Prices and Levels renames them, sets their percentage and switches them on or off). **Set Item Prices and Levels** puts the default price and every price level of every item (or one type or category) in a single grid, so a whole price list is set and saved in one pass; a column can be filled from the cost, from the default price, or from each level's own percentage. **Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand** offers two ways in: **Per Item Adjustment**, the QuickBooks layout (pick items on lines, or many at once with **Find & Select Items**, type the new quantity or the difference, with the U/M and a class on each line, a Customer:Job, **Set Qty to Zero**, and the total value and number of item adjustments below), and **Per Batch Adjustment**, the grid of every item of the site, narrowed to one or several **categories** ticked in a drop-down list; the choice is remembered. The **Inventory Center** has a search box that narrows the item list as you type and a **Site** filter: with a site chosen, the quantities, the open documents and the movement history shown are that site's alone. The inventory reports include **No. of Days Inventory Has No Movement** (days since each item was last sold to a customer, with its last movement of any kind, by site and category), **Quantity on Hand by Site**, the **Negative Inventory Listing** and the **Physical Inventory Worksheet** as of any date, the **Customized Inventory Report** (one line per item with the columns each user picks: item details, stock and value, gross profit per unit and gross profit %, and the sales, cost of sales, gross profit and purchases of a period), **Inventory Valuation Summary** for one inventory site or all, and the **Inventory Movement Summary Report** and **Inventory Movement Detailed Report** (beginning balance, what came in and what went out by kind of transaction, and the ending balance of each item over a period, by site and class, every figure drilled down to its source transactions). Processing orders print the total of their inputs, the total of their outputs and the variance between the two. The transfer list shows the From and To sites of every transfer, and the printed transfer names both. Costing is weighted-average per item; a sale that brings an item to zero takes all of the remaining value to Cost of Goods Sold. Processing orders show a running total of the allocated cost. Includes valuation and stock-status reports.

## 6. How access control works

1. A user can open a page if **any of their roles** allows it, unless a **user override** says otherwise. Overrides always win.
2. On each page, roles grant actions separately: create, edit, delete/void, print, approve.
3. **Administrator** roles can open everything.
4. Users restricted to certain **sites** or **POS terminals** can only post to those, and lists, site pickers and site-based reports show only their sites.
5. **Supervisor approval:** actions beyond a user's limits need another user who has *approve* on that page. Examples are voids, refunds, large discounts, price overrides, and amounts above the user's maximum. The approver types their username and password on the same form, or the user types a one-time **approval code** the approver generated on Point of Sale → Approval Codes (see § 5). You cannot approve your own transaction, nor use a code you generated yourself.
6. Every denied page, failed approval, and permission change is written to the audit trail.
7. With several companies (§ 13), each company has a **Company Owner**, who can always open every page of that company whatever the roles and overrides say, and whose account only the owner (or the Super Admin) can change. A **Super Admin** belongs to no company: they manage the companies from the platform pages and can open any company to work inside it. Nobody can reach another company's records: the company a session works on is decided on the server when the user logs in, and nothing in the address, a form or a cookie can change it.

- **`config/config.php`:** database connection, company time zone (default `Asia/Manila`), currency symbol, and debug mode. Set the environment variable `APP_DEBUG=1` only while troubleshooting.
- **System Administration → Company, Security & Closing Date:** company details, password rules, lockout, session timeout, finance-charge rate, negative-stock rule, and closing date.
- **Financial → Module Default Accounts:** which accounts each module posts to.
- **Scheduled report e-mails:** run this once a day. It uses PHP `mail()`, so configure SMTP in `php.ini` (XAMPP: `sendmail.ini`).
  - Linux cron: `0 6 * * * php /path/to/accounting-system/tools/send_scheduled_reports.php`
  - Windows Task Scheduler: `C:\xampp\php\php.exe C:\xampp\htdocs\accounting-system\tools\send_scheduled_reports.php`
- **Fixed share capital bills (Water Billing):** the bills of Fixed Share Capital Billing are posted when their day comes, whenever that page or the cashier opens. To post them early each morning anyway, run `php water/process/runShareCapitalBilling.php` once a day (Windows Task Scheduler: `C:\xampp\php\php.exe C:\xampp\htdocs\accounting-system\water\process\runShareCapitalBilling.php`). It is safe to run any number of times and makes no journal entry.
- **Bluetooth receipt printer:** **System Administration → Company, Security & Closing Date → Bluetooth receipt printer** turns on a *Bluetooth printer* button beside *Print* on every receipt. Choose the paper width (32 characters for a 58 mm roll, which is what the Officom OC-PT210 takes; 42 or 48 for an 80 mm roll), how many blank lines to feed, whether the printer cuts the paper, and whether a receipt prints the moment it opens. *Test print* on the same page pairs the printer and checks the width. The browser does the talking, which puts two conditions on it and neither can be worked around from inside the program: it must be **Chrome, Edge, Opera or Samsung Internet on Android**, or Chrome or Edge on a computer (Firefox, iPhone and iPad, and the browsers built into Facebook and Messenger cannot reach Bluetooth at all), and the page must be opened on an **`https://`** address or on **`http://localhost/`**. Opened on a plain address such as `http://192.168.1.10/` the browser refuses, and the receipt page says so in plain words rather than failing quietly. The simplest way to get `https://` on a local network is a self-signed certificate in Apache, or a tunnel; until then, printing from the till itself at `http://localhost/accounting-system/` works.
- **Default site & class for POS transactions:** **System Administration → Company, Security & Closing Date → Default site & class for POS transactions** lists every user with a Default site and a Default class box. - **Phones and tablets:** every page is laid out for a small screen. The menu becomes a panel behind the button at the top left, a table wider than the screen is swiped sideways inside its own box (with the edge shaded while there are columns still out of sight) instead of dragging the whole page with it (each report line keeps to one line so it can be followed across, and only long memos and descriptions wrap), boxes and buttons are large enough to tap, and text boxes are set at a size that stops a phone zooming in when one is tapped. Searchable boxes are drawn in the part of the screen the on-screen keyboard is not covering, so the choices stay in view while typing, on a phone held either way up.

Whichever you use, keep a copy off the server.
- **Receipts:** uploads are stored in `storage/receipts/`, which the web server cannot serve directly; files are sent only to logged-in users with access. It also holds the company logo (`storage/company/`), the maintenance pictures (`storage/maintenance/`) and the database backups (`storage/backups/`); include it in your backups. With several companies, the first company keeps these folders and every other company has its own under `storage/companies/c002/`, `c003/` and so on; `storage/platform/` holds the switch that turns multi-company on.

## 10. Accounting notes

- Prices and amounts on sales and purchase forms are **VAT-exclusive**; VAT (12% by default) is added per line and posted to Output Tax or Input Tax.
- Inventory uses **weighted-average cost** per item across all sites. The average is always book value divided by quantity on hand, so rounding never leaves a value behind when the quantity reaches zero. Quantities are tracked per site. Selling below zero is allowed unless you turn it off in settings; when it is allowed, a sales invoice, sales receipt, batch of invoices, POS sale, inventory adjustment or transfer that would take an item below zero at a site first asks "Saving the transaction will lead the inventory to a negative balance. Do you want to continue?" (Yes saves, No goes back to the form). Negative stock is listed in the *Negative Inventory Listing* report.
- **Withholding taxes** are worked out on the amount **net of VAT** (a bill of P112,000 with 12% VAT is a base of P100,000: 1% is P1,000, 5% is P5,000). A bill payment debits Accounts Payable with the bill amount settled and credits the bank with the cash paid and **Withholding Tax Payable** (1% / 2%, remitted with 0619-E and 1601-EQ) and **VAT Withheld Payable** (5%, remitted with 1600-VT) with the taxes withheld. On a customer payment the 1% / 2% go to **Creditable Withholding Tax** (an income-tax credit) and the 5% to **Creditable VAT Withheld** (a VAT credit, 2550Q Item 16), never to Input Tax. An **advance to a supplier** debits Advances to Suppliers; applying it to a bill debits Accounts Payable and credits Advances to Suppliers.
- Estimates, sales orders, purchase orders, pending invoices/builds, and suspended POS sales **do not post** to the ledger.
- **Void** keeps the document with zero amounts. **Delete** removes it. Both require a reason and are recorded in full in the audit trail. Deleting or voiding is blocked while payments are applied.
- Transactions dated on or before the **closing date**, and reconciled bank lines, can only be changed by users with the *closed-period changes* permission. Changes to reconciled amounts are listed in the Reconciliation Discrepancy report.
- The sample database is dated around **September 2026** (closing date 06/28/2026).
- For MySQL 5.5 compatibility, the automatic "created" date columns (`created_at`, `opened_at`, and so on) are `TIMESTAMP` columns, the only type MySQL 5.5 fills in automatically. That type is limited to dates up to 2038-01-19. Business dates (transaction, due, and acquisition dates) are ordinary `DATE` columns with no such limit. `updated_at` is filled in by the application.

## 12. Before going live

- Import `accounting_system_clean.sql` (or run `php tools/install.php` without `--demo`).
- Change the admin password, and deactivate the demo users you don't need.
- Create a MySQL user just for this app (instead of `root`) and put it in `config/config.php`.
- Use HTTPS. The session cookie becomes secure automatically.
- Keep `APP_DEBUG` off.
- Set your company details, the closing date, and the module default accounts (including the Water group).
- Set up the classes (Financial → Class List): the cooperative teller, savings and loan forms and every Water Billing form that posts are not saved without a class.
- Water Billing: institution name and signatories, collection policy (penalty and compromise income), bill code accounts (A/R must be an Accounts Receivable account), the cash account of every cashier, and a billing period. The Account Mapping Validation report lists anything still missing.
- Make a backup before each month-end close, and download a copy off the server (System Administration → Backup & Restore).
- With several companies: keep the Super Admin's password safe (a second Super Admin is a good idea), check that each company has a Company Owner, and back up the platform database (`accounting_system_master`) with the company databases.

## 13. Several companies, each with its own database

The system can run any number of companies. Each company has a **database of its own**, so its records can never be seen, changed or reported on by another company. The database the system was installed with stays exactly as it is and becomes **Company 1**.

**Roles.** The **Super Admin** manages the platform: every company, its owner, its Company Code and its database. Each company has a **Company Owner**, who manages that company's users: approving or rejecting employees who join, creating and editing users, giving them roles, switching them off and on, resetting passwords, and the Company Code. Below the owner come the company's administrators and employees, with the roles and page access the company already had.

**Setting it up (once).** Run the migration `2026_09_28_multi_company.sql` on your database as usual, log in as an administrator and open **System Administration → Multi-Company Setup**. Choose the Company Owner of this company from its users, choose the Super Admin's username and password, tick that a backup was made, and press **Set up**. The system then:

- creates the platform (master) database, `accounting_system_master` (your database name followed by `_master`; `config/config.php` can name another one with `'master' => ['name' => '...']`), with the same MySQL user as `config/config.php`;
- registers your database as Company 1 without moving, changing or deleting any record in it, and puts back the audit trail protection triggers if an earlier restore had removed them;
- lists every existing user of Company 1 on the platform, so all of them keep logging in with the same username and password;
- gives Company 1 its Company Code and the owner you chose;
- creates the Super Admin, who logs in on the same log-in page and lands on the platform pages.

**The platform pages (Super Admin).** **Companies** lists every company with its code, owner, status, number of users, waiting employees and database. **Open** works inside a company as the Super Admin (a yellow strip says so, and every change is in that company's audit trail under an account of the Super Admin's own that nobody can log into). **Details** shows and edits the company's information, assigns or changes the Company Owner (one of its users, or a new account), regenerates the code, switches joining off or sets an expiry date, changes whether employees need approval, shows the company's users, its database (name, tables, audit trail protection, pending updates, file folder), the set-up steps and the platform activity, and switches the company off or on. **Database updates** runs the upgrade scripts of a new version on every company database. **Super Admins**, **Platform settings** (the name prefix of new databases, the approval default, session and log-in limits) and the **Platform audit log** complete it.

**Creating a company.** **New company** asks for the company's details, the company whose set-up is copied (Company 1 unless another is chosen), whether employees need approval, and the owner's account (or the owner is assigned later). Pressing **Create company**:

1. generates a unique Company Code;
2. names a database for it (`accounting_system_company_002`, `_003` ...; a name that already exists on the server is never used);
3. creates the database and every table exactly as the copied company has them today, with all columns, keys, indexes and foreign keys, and the counters starting from 1;
4. copies the set-up lists only: settings (with the new company's name, address, phone, e-mail and TIN; no logo, no closing date), the modules, pages, roles with their page access and special permissions, the numbering (starting again from 1), the chart of accounts (without bank account numbers), module default accounts, terms, tax codes, payment methods, price level names, customer types, billing groups, item categories, units of measure and fixed asset categories; and adds one inventory site, Main;
5. puts the audit trail protection triggers in place;
6. creates the owner's account with the company's full-access role (the owner must change the temporary password at the first log-in);
7. checks the new database (every table there, the structure matching the program, no customer, supplier, item or transaction in it);
8. registers the company, its database and its users on the platform and creates its file folder;
9. and only then switches the company on.

Nothing else is copied: no customers, suppliers, items, employees or other names, users, transactions, sales, payments, bills, journals, balances, budgets, fixed assets, POS records, audit trail or history. Every step is logged. If one fails, the company is marked **Set-up failed** with the step and the reason, the database that attempt created is dropped again, nobody can log in to it or join it, and the Super Admin can set it up again once the cause is fixed, or delete the failed record.

**The Company Code and joining.** Every company has a code such as `ABD-7K2QX`: its initials and five random characters, unique on the platform and never reused, saying nothing about the database. The owner hands it out; new employees open **Join your company** from the log-in page (`register.php`) and enter their full name, e-mail, employee number (optional), username, password and the code. The code is checked on the server; a wrong, old, switched-off or expired code, or the code of an inactive company, all get the same message ("Invalid or inactive Company Code. Please verify the code with your Company Owner."), and ten wrong codes from one address stop that address for 15 minutes. The account is created in that company's database only and, unless the company switched approval off, waits for approval: the employee is told so when trying to log in, and the owner finds the request under **Company → Join Requests** (the menu shows how many are waiting) to approve it with a role or reject it with a reason. The code is never a password: it only says which company; the employee always logs in with their own username and password. Regenerating the code (owner or Super Admin) retires the old one at once; everyone who already joined keeps their account.

**The Company menu (owner).** **Company Profile & Code** shows the company, its owner, its users by status and the code with *Copy code* and *Copy join link*; the owner can regenerate it, switch joining off, set an expiry date, and choose whether new employees need approval and the role they start with (a full-access role is never given without approval). **Employees & Users** lists the users with tabs for all, join requests, active, inactive, locked and rejected, a search box and a role filter, and shows each person's roles, status, how the account was made (by an administrator, with the Company Code, with the company, by the Super Admin), when, and the last log-in. From there the owner approves or rejects, changes roles, resets a password, unlocks, deactivates and activates; *New user* and the full details (sites, POS terminals) are the usual User Accounts page. Company administrators can use these pages too, but only the owner can change the code or the joining rules, and nobody but the owner or the Super Admin can change the owner's account.

**Logging in.** Usernames are unique across all companies and the Super Admins. The log-in page looks the username up on the platform, which says which company it belongs to; the company has to be active; the password, lock-out, approval and password rules are then checked in that company's own database, and the session is tied to that company. On every click the system checks again that the company is still active and that the user still belongs to it. A company switched off by the Super Admin logs its users out at their next click and keeps its database, users and records until it is switched on again.

**One user, two companies.** A user of one company can also work in another company with the same log-in, without being a Super Admin. An administrator of the other company opens **User Accounts → Add user from another company**, types the person's username and gives them roles, sites and POS terminals in that company; the platform keeps the link (`user_company_links`, created by itself the first time) and that company holds a linked account that never logs in by itself. The person logs in at their own company as always; the company name in the top bar becomes a switch between their companies, and a blue strip shows while they work in the other one. On every click the link, the person's own account and both companies are checked again. Either company can remove the access (the linked account is switched off, so its name stays on what it entered), and it ends when the person's own account is deactivated. Their password and password rules stay those of their own company; their work and every switch are in the audit trail of the company they work in.

**Isolation.** The company database is chosen in one place only (`app/Tenant.php`), from the platform's records and the server-side session, never from the address, a form or a cookie; with several companies and none chosen, the program refuses to read any company database. Database names are made by the system and never typed by anyone. Each company's logo, pictures, receipts and backups are kept in its own folder. A restore on the Backup & Restore page can only rebuild the company's own tables: statements that switch to or name another database, create databases, users, triggers, routines or views, read files or copy data with a SELECT are refused and listed, and the audit trail protection is put back afterwards. Backup files of the other companies are named after the company (`company_002_...`), not after the database.

**What the MySQL user needs.** The user in `config/config.php` creates and drops the company databases, so it needs CREATE and DROP on databases, and CREATE, ALTER, INDEX, REFERENCES, INSERT, SELECT, UPDATE, DELETE and TRIGGER in them (on MySQL 5.5 with binary logging switched on, creating triggers also needs SUPER, or `log_bin_trust_function_creators = 1`). XAMPP's `root` has all of them. On shared hosting where databases can only be made in the control panel, create the platform database there first (and give the user full rights on it); new companies then need the same treatment and a database name prefix set in **Platform settings** (for example `cpuser_company_`).

**Maintenance.** `tools/send_scheduled_reports.php` sends every active company's scheduled reports from the same cron line (or one company with `--company=N`). `php tools/platform.php list | reset-superadmin <username> <password> | sync <company id> | update-all` lists the companies, resets a Super Admin's password, rebuilds a company's list of usernames, and runs pending upgrade scripts on every company database. To switch multi-company off in an emergency, move `storage/platform/multi_company.json` away: the system runs as Company 1 alone with its own users; put the file back to return. Back up the platform database together with the company databases.

## 14. User's Manual

The program has its manual built in: 16 chapters with a section for every page, form and report, and a screenshot of each form. Chapter 13, Cooperative Management, covers the cooperative module, and chapter 14, Parties and Lines of Business, the Party Master, the lines of business and the Consolidated Party Statement. Every page has a **Help** button at the right of its title bar that opens the manual at that page's section; the manual is also in the user menu (**User's Manual**), and linked from the log-in page and the join page. The manual has its own search, a contents list, a list of the sections of the chapter you are reading, click-to-enlarge screenshots and **Print this chapter**, and it works on phones.

Chapters 1 (Getting Started) and 12 (joining a company) can be read before logging in; the other chapters need a log-in (into a company, or as the Super Admin). The manual needs no database table: it is `manual.php`, `app/Manual.php`, `views/manual/` and `assets/manual/`. The same manual is supplied as a Word document (`G-SMaRT_Accounting_Users_Manual.docx`) for printing and for reading away from the program; the Word edition does not include the cooperative chapters 13 and 14 yet (use **Print this chapter** in the built-in manual).
