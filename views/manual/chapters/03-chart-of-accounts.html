The Financial module (called Chart of Accounts before version 39) holds the accounts that every transaction posts to, the lists the other modules share, the journal entries you make by hand, and the financial statements. Most entries reach the general ledger on their own: an invoice, a bill, a check or a POS sale writes its ledger lines the moment it is saved. This chapter explains how to set the accounts up, how to record what the modules cannot post for you, and how to read the ledger and the statements.

In the sidebar the module has four groups:

  • Lists: Chart of Accounts, Account Types, Class List, Employees & Other Names, Memorized Transactions.
  • Setup: Set Up Budgets, Module Default Accounts, Terms, Tax Codes & Other Lists.
  • Transactions: Make Journal Entries.
  • Reports: the trial balances, balance sheets, income statements, the comparative income statements and balance sheets (by month, quarter, year and your own ranges or dates), cash flow, ledgers and journal. Click Reports to open the list. The Report Center lists them in its Financial Reports card.
Note

You see only the pages and buttons your role allows (see Page Access Configuration). Adding, changing or deleting accounts and changing the module default accounts also needs the special permission Can change the Chart of Accounts and default accounts, set in User Role Configuration.

Chart of Accounts

The Chart of Accounts is the list of every account the books use: cash and bank accounts, receivables, inventory, payables, equity, income and expenses. Here you add accounts, group them under main accounts, give them their account type, mark the ones that keep a subsidiary ledger, and open the ledger or the register of any account.

Where to find it: Financial > Chart of Accounts

The accounts are shown in five cards, Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Incomes and Expenses. Each card is in code order, and every main account is followed by its sub-accounts. A sub-account is indented under its main account with an arrow, for example 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking under 1000 Cash, and a main account says under its name how many sub-accounts it has. Accounts that the modules need, such as 1100 Accounts Receivable, 1200 Inventory and 2000 Accounts Payable, carry the word system in grey. Tick Include inactive accounts above the list to show the accounts you have made inactive; they appear greyed out.

Step by step

To add an account:

  1. Open Financial > Chart of Accounts.
  2. Click New account.
  3. Choose the Account type.
  4. Type the Code and the Name.
  5. If the account belongs under another one, choose it in Sub-account of. The new account takes the type of that main account unless you chose a type yourself.
  6. Leave Normal balance at From type unless this is a contra account.
  7. Tick With SL if you want the account to keep a subsidiary ledger.
  8. For a bank account, type the Bank account no.
  9. Click Save account, or Save account & New to save it and start another one straight away.

To change an account:

  1. Click Edit on the account's line.
  2. Change what you need. The title of the form shows the code, for example "Edit account 1000".
  3. Click Save account.

To make an account inactive:

  1. Click Edit on the account's line.
  2. Untick Active.
  3. Click Save account.

To delete an account:

  1. Find the account. A Delete link appears only on an account that is not a system account, whose balance is zero and that has no sub-accounts.
  2. Click Delete and confirm "Delete this account?".

To open an account's register: click Register on its line. The link appears for Bank, Credit Card, Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, Other Current Asset and Other Current Liability accounts. See Use Register.

To export the list: click Export. The file chart_of_accounts.csv holds, for every account shown and in the order of the list, the code, name, type, the built-in type it behaves as, category, normal balance, parent, With SL, balance, the total with sub-accounts (for a main account that has sub-accounts) and the active flag. Export needs the print/export permission on this page.

Buttons

  • New account: opens a blank account form. Shown only to users with the special permission to change the Chart of Accounts.
  • Account types: opens Account Types. The account form has the same button.
  • Export: downloads the list as a spreadsheet file.
  • Module default accounts: opens Module Default Accounts.

With SL: keeping a subsidiary ledger

An account marked With SL keeps a listing of the names that transacted it, like a subsidiary ledger of advances to employees, deposits from customers or loans from officers. While the box is ticked:

  • every journal entry, cash voucher and check that posts to the account must name a customer, supplier, employee or other name. On a journal entry or cash voucher the name is chosen on the line; on a check, the payee of the check counts for every line.
  • saving without a name is refused with a message that names the account, for example "The account 1400 - Prepaid Expenses is marked "With SL", so the line posting to it needs a name: choose a customer, supplier, employee or other name."
  • the Subsidiary Ledger and the Schedule of Accounts list the account and its names.

The rule applies to entries saved after the box is ticked. Entries made earlier keep whatever name they had.

What it records

Adding or changing an account posts nothing. Every change is written to the audit trail.

Good to know

  • "Enter the account code and name." Both are required.
  • "Another account uses this code." Choose a different code.
  • "A sub-account must be under a parent account of the same category." Choose a parent of the same category, or change the type.
  • "System accounts used by the modules cannot be made inactive." Accounts with the system tag must stay active.
  • "This account has transactions; it cannot be moved to a different category." You can change the type within the same category (for example from Expense to Other Expense), but not from an expense to an asset once entries have been posted.
  • "This account has sub-accounts of its category; it cannot be moved to another category." A main account keeps its category while it has sub-accounts.
  • "This account is one the modules post to: it can only take a type that behaves as Accounts Receivable." An account with the system word keeps its built-in type. It can still take a type of your own that behaves as the same built-in type, for example 1050 Undeposited Funds a type that behaves as Other Current Asset.
  • "This account is in use. Make it inactive instead of deleting it." An account cannot be deleted while it has ledger entries or transaction lines, is a module default account, has sub-accounts, or is used on an item. Make it inactive instead.
  • Without the special permission to change the Chart of Accounts, the list is read-only and the New account, Edit and Delete links do not appear.

Account Types

Account Types lists the account types: the fifteen built-in ones and the types you add yourself, such as Cash or Loans Receivable. Each type is a classification of its own on the Balance Sheet and the Income Statement (a heading, its accounts and a total), and each balance sheet type is linked to a section of the Statement of Cash Flows, so the cash flow statement always ties to the cash accounts.

Where to find it: Financial > Account Types, or the Account types button on the Chart of Accounts and on the account form.

A type of your own behaves as one of the built-in types. The built-in type decides everything the program does with the accounts: their category and normal balance, the group they are shown in (Current Assets, Fixed Assets, Expenses ...) and which forms offer them. An account of the type Cash that behaves as Bank is offered on Write Checks, Make Deposits, the cash vouchers, the teller cash and Reconcile, exactly like a Bank account. Your type adds its own name, its place on the statements and its section of the Statement of Cash Flows.

The types are shown in five cards, Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Income and Expenses, in the order they take on the statements.

The accounts of a type

An open type has two more cards:

  • Accounts of this type lists its accounts. Tick some, choose another type in Move the ticked accounts to and click Move.
  • Give accounts this type lists the accounts of the same category that are of another type, each with its present type. Type part of a code or name in the search box to narrow the list. Ticking a main account ticks its sub-accounts too; untick any you want to leave. Click Give them the type and the type's name.

An account that takes a type of another built-in type behaves as that built-in type from then on: for example an Other Current Asset account given the type Cash (which behaves as Bank) is then offered wherever money is paid or received. Its entries do not change. Accounts the modules post to (with the word system on the Chart of Accounts) are offered only for a type that behaves as their own built-in type. You can also choose the type of one account on its form in the Chart of Accounts.

Step by step

To add a type, for example Loans Receivable:

  1. Open Financial > Account Types and click New account type.
  2. Type the Name: Loans Receivable.
  3. In Behaves as, choose Other Current Asset.
  4. Leave On the Statement of Cash Flows at Operating activities.
  5. Type an Order (5 puts it before the other current assets) and a Description if you like.
  6. Click Save account type. The message "Account type added. Give it its accounts below." appears and the type stays open.

To give accounts a type:

  1. Open the type (Edit on its line).
  2. In Give accounts this type, tick the accounts, for example 1210 Loans Receivable - Current, 1211 Loans Receivable - Past Due and the others of the loan portfolio.
  3. Click Give them the type Loans Receivable. The message says how many accounts are now of the type.

To make a Cash classification of the cash on hand, the cash in banks and the cash funds: add a type named Cash that behaves as Bank, with Cash and cash equivalents on the Statement of Cash Flows, then give it 1000 Cash (its sub-accounts are ticked with it) and any other cash account. The Balance Sheet then shows the heading Cash with these accounts and Total Cash, and the Statement of Cash Flows ends with the same total.

To move accounts to another type: open the type they are of, tick them under Accounts of this type, choose the other type in Move the ticked accounts to and click Move. Only types of the same category are offered.

To change a type: open it, change what you need and click Save account type. The statements follow at once; no entry changes.

To delete a type: open a type of yours that no account uses and click Delete this type, then confirm. A type with accounts must give them to another type first.

How the statements show the types

  • Balance Sheet and Income Statement: inside the group of its built-in type (Current Assets, Fixed Assets, Expenses ...), every type has a heading with its name, its accounts and the line Total and the type's name. Where a group holds only one built-in type and none of yours, the group's own heading is enough and the type heading is left out, for example Fixed Assets.
  • Main accounts and sub-accounts: a sub-account is shown under its main account, in the classification of the main account's type. On the Balance Sheet and the Income Statement a main account with sub-accounts is a heading followed by its sub-accounts, a line for its own entries when it has any, and its total. The Balance Sheet Summary and the Income Statement Summary show the main accounts only, each with its total.
  • Statement of Cash Flows: the accounts of the types in Cash and cash equivalents give the cash at the beginning and at the end of the period. The change in every other balance sheet account is shown in the section of its type, under the type's heading with the line Net change in and the type's name. Each account is counted once, so the cash at the end of the period equals the total of the cash accounts on the Balance Sheet.

What it records

Adding or changing a type and giving accounts a type post nothing and change no amount. Every change is written to the audit trail; giving an account a type is written as a change of that account.

Good to know

  • "There is already an account type named "Cash"." Names are compared without regard to capitals.
  • "Choose the section of the Statement of Cash Flows the accounts of this type belong to." An asset, liability or equity type needs its section.
  • "5 account(s) are of this type. Move them to another type first." A type with accounts cannot be deleted, and the built-in type it behaves as cannot change.
  • "The account 1100 is one the modules post to: it can only take a type that behaves as Accounts Receivable." Such accounts keep their built-in type.
  • A sub-account keeps the category of its main account, and an account with entries keeps its category, so types are given within one category.
  • Adding, changing and deleting types needs the special permission Can change the Chart of Accounts and default accounts; giving accounts a type also needs Edit on the Chart of Accounts. Users without it see the list only.
  • Import Data accepts the name of any type, built-in or yours, in the account type column of the accounts file.

Every transaction form has a Class box, the class is copied to every ledger line the transaction posts, and the financial reports can be filtered by class or shown with one column per class. Good to know

It will be saved as Unclassified and will not appear under any class on the class reports." Click Choose a class to go back to the box, or Continue saving. The question is not asked while no class exists yet.
  • On journal entries and cash vouchers each line can carry its own class. The class at the top of the form fills the class of every line that has an account.

Memorized Transactions

A memorized transaction is a saved copy of a transaction you enter again and again: the monthly rent check, a standing invoice, the payroll journal entry. It keeps the names, accounts, items and amounts; the date and the document number are given each time it is entered. You can enter it from the list whenever you need it, get a reminder when it is due, or let the program enter it on its due date.

Where to find it: Financial > Memorized Transactions. The Home page also shows Memorized transactions due with an Open button when a reminder is due (see Home Dashboard).

The columns

Column What it shows
Name and type The name you gave it, with its type (Invoice, Bill, Check, Journal Entry, Inventory Adjustment and so on) and its notes underneath.
Name on the transaction The customer, supplier or payee of the saved copy.
Amount The total of the saved copy.
Schedule How it is entered: Automatically, Reminder, When needed or, for an adjustment or a batch of invoices, Loaded when needed (the full wording shows when you point at it), with the frequency under it, for example "Every month" or "Every 2 weeks".

Memorizing a transaction

You add to this list in two ways, for users who may create memorized transactions:

  • From the form, before saving: Memorize Transaction beside the save buttons of every transaction form (see Memorize Transaction and Load Memorized Transaction), and Memorize Adjustment on Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand. Item receipts, batches of invoices and inventory adjustments are memorized this way.
  • From a saved transaction: the Memorize button on the saved (not voided) invoices, sales receipts, estimates, sales orders, statement charges, credit memos, bills, supplier debit memos, purchase orders, checks, journal entries and cash vouchers.

Load Memorized Transaction on a new form fills the form from one of them. On this list, the Open button (open on the form) does the same: it opens a new form of its kind filled from it.

The three ways of entering:

  • Enter it automatically: the program records the entry on its due date, the first time anyone opens the system that day, and shows "Memorized transaction "..." was entered automatically for 09/03/2026." If several due dates were missed, it catches up one date at a time, each entry with its own date.
  • Remind me when it is due: from the set number of days before the due date, the line shows Due (or Overdue once the date has passed) and the Home page lists it. You enter it with Enter now.
  • Don't remind me (I enter it from the list): it waits on the list until you enter it.

Step by step

To memorize a transaction:

  1. Open the saved transaction, for example a bill from Accounting Partners & Co.
  2. Click Memorize.
  3. Check Name it and choose a Group if you use groups.
  4. Choose How it is entered.
  5. Set Every, for example 1 Month(s), and the Next due date.
  6. Set Remind (days before) and Number remaining if needed.
  7. Click Memorize. The message "Memorized as "...". It is on the Memorized Transactions list." confirms it.

To enter a memorized transaction now:

  1. Open Financial > Memorized Transactions.
  2. Click Enter now on its line. The dialog Enter a memorized transaction opens.
  3. Check the Date of the entry. It starts at the next due date.
  4. Leave Move the schedule on to the next due date ticked if this entry is the one that was due. Untick it for an extra entry that should not move the schedule.
  5. Click Enter it. The document is recorded straight away and opens so you can check it.

To change the schedule:

  1. Click the pencil button on the line.
  2. In the Edit form at the top, change the Name, Group (optional), How it is entered, Every, Next due, Remind (days before), Number remaining (blank = no limit), Stop after, Active or Notes.
  3. Click Save.

To take one off the list: click the trash button and confirm "Take "..." off the memorized list? The transactions already entered from it are not touched."

Buttons

  • New journal entry opens a blank Make Journal Entries form.
  • Enter now is shown on active lines to users who may create memorized transactions. It is green when the line is due.
  • Open opens a new form filled from the line, to change and save. A batch of invoices and an inventory adjustment are always used this way (their figures are revised each time), so they have no Enter now; their button is green when they are due.

What it records

Entering a memorized transaction saves an ordinary transaction of its type, with a new number and the date you chose, and posts it exactly as if you had typed it. An invoice or bill gets its due date from its terms. After the entry, Last entered, Entered and Number remaining are updated and Next due moves on by the frequency. Each entry is noted in the audit trail.

Good to know

  • The saved copy does not change when you edit the original transaction later. To change the amounts, memorize the corrected transaction again and delete the old line.
  • When Number remaining reaches zero, or the next due date falls after Stop after, the memorized transaction becomes inactive and is no longer entered or reminded.
  • An automatic entry that cannot be recorded (for example because its date is on or before the closing date, or a default account is missing) is switched to Remind me when it is due and the reason is written to the audit trail. Enter it by hand once the problem is fixed.
  • Opening balance entries and voided transactions cannot be memorized.
  • The transactions entered from the list are ordinary transactions: change, void or delete them on their own pages.

Make Journal Entries

A journal entry posts debits and credits directly to the accounts you choose. Routine activity posts automatically from invoices, bills, payments and the POS, so you use journal entries for what no other form records: opening balances, accruals, reclassifications, corrections and closing entries.

Where to find it: Financial > Make Journal Entries opens the list of journal entries. The Home page quick action Make Journal Entries opens a blank entry.

The list shows the journal entries and opening balance entries of a date range (from 90 days back to 30 days ahead when it opens). Use From, To, Status (Posted or Void), Class and Search (number, name or memo), then click Filter. Click a number to open the entry. Export downloads the list.

Step by step

To make a journal entry:

  1. Open Financial > Make Journal Entries and click New journal entry.
  2. Check the Date.
  3. Type a Reference and a Memo.
  4. Choose the Class if you use classes.
  5. On the first line, choose the Account and type the Debit.
  6. On the next line, choose the account and type the Credit.
  7. Add more lines if needed, until Difference shows 0.00.
  8. Choose a Name on every line that needs one.
  9. Click Save entry, or Save entry & New to start another one.
Example

To accrue September rent of ₱85,000.00 that will be billed in October, debit 6010 Rent Expense ₱85,000.00 and credit 2200 Accrued Expenses ₱85,000.00, with the memo "Accrued rent, September".

To enter opening balances:

  1. Start a new journal entry dated the day before you begin using the system.
  2. Tick Opening balance entry.
  3. Debit each asset account with its balance and credit each liability and equity account with its balance.
  4. Save when Difference is 0.00. In the demo company, OB-0001 debits 1010 Cash in Bank - BDO Checking ₱2,500,000.00 and the other cash accounts, and credits 3000 Owner's Capital ₱3,320,000.00.

To change a journal entry: a saved entry is changed only with the approval of a second, authorized user (a user with the Approve right on Make Journal Entries).

  1. Open the entry from the list.
  2. Click Edit (needs approval).
  3. Change the lines or the header.
  4. Type the Explanation: why the entry is changed.
  5. If the approver is with you, open Supervisor approval and let them type their user name and password (or an approval code); the change is made at once. Otherwise leave it empty.
  6. Click Save the change. Without an approver at the desk the change waits on Journal Entry Changes, and the entry stays as it is until it is approved.

An entry with a change waiting shows a yellow notice with Review the change, and an entry that was changed lists its changes under Changes made to this entry.

To print a journal entry: open it and click Print, then click Print above the page. See the print format below.

To void or delete a journal entry: open it, click Void or Delete, type the reason and confirm. Voiding keeps the entry with no effect on the ledger; deleting removes it, and a full copy stays in the audit trail.

To memorize a journal entry: open it and click Memorize (see Memorized Transactions).

The saved entry shows the date, number, status, memo, class and who entered it, the lines as you entered them, and General ledger entries created automatically with the total debits and credits. A ledger line that was cleared in a bank reconciliation is marked cleared.

Buttons

  • Edit, Print, Memorize, Void, Delete: see the procedures above. Each appears only when you have the permission.
  • History opens the Transaction History of the entry.
  • Back to list returns to the Journal Entries list.
  • Journal list (on the form) goes back to the list without saving.

The print format

The printed journal entry has the company name, address and TIN (and the logo, when the administrator has switched it on for this document), the title JOURNAL ENTRY or OPENING BALANCE, the number and the date. Under it are the memo, reference, site and class, then a table with Account, Description, Name, Class, Debit and Credit, the two totals, and the signature lines Prepared by (the user who entered it), Checked by and Approved by. A line without its own class prints the class of the entry.

What it records

Each line posts to its account exactly as entered: a debit line debits the account, a credit line credits it. The name and the class of the line go with it to the ledger, so a line to 1100 Accounts Receivable with the name ABC Trading Corp. changes that customer's balance. An opening balance entry posts the same way; only its label differs.

Good to know

  • "A journal entry needs at least two lines."
  • "Debits (85,000.00) must equal credits (80,000.00)." The totals must agree to the centavo.
  • "Choose an account for every journal line." A line with an amount needs an account.
  • "A journal line cannot have both a debit and a credit." Split it into two lines.
  • "Lines posted to Accounts Receivable need a customer name." and "Lines posted to Accounts Payable need a vendor name." Choose the name on that line.
  • A journal entry to Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable changes the customer's or supplier's balance, but it is not an invoice or a bill: it does not appear in Receive Payments or Pay Bills. Use those modules for sales and purchases.
  • A journal entry never changes item quantities, even when it posts to 1200 Inventory. Use Adjust Quantity/Value on Hand for stock.
  • "The books are closed through 06/28/2026. You do not have permission to record transactions dated on or before the closing date." Only users with the special permission Can change transactions on/before the closing date or already reconciled can post on or before the closing date, or change an entry that was cleared in a bank reconciliation.
  • A voided entry cannot be edited.
  • When the Class at the top is empty but every line that has an account carries its own class, the program saves without asking about the class.
  • Journal entry numbers (JE-) are shared with other entries the program makes itself, such as bank service charges, interest earned and cash over and short, so the numbers on this list may skip.

Journal Entry Changes

The changes to saved journal entries (and opening balance entries) that wait for approval, and the ones decided. A change is made only when a second user approves it: the maker asks, with the explanation; the checker compares and approves or rejects.

Where to find it: Financial > Journal Entry Changes, or Journal entry changes on the journal entry form. Users without the Approve right on Make Journal Entries see their own requests.

The list has the tabs Waiting for approval, Approved, Rejected, Withdrawn and All, with who asked and when, the entry, the explanation, the status and who decided. Click Review (or Open) to see one change.

Reviewing a change

The change shows the entry, who asked, when and why, and Before and after: the entry as it is now and the entry as proposed, line by line, with the values the change alters highlighted. A change of the date, number, reference, memo, class or line of business is listed above the lines.

  • Approve and apply saves the entry with the proposed lines at once, through the same checks as a new entry (balanced debits and credits, the closing date), with the explanation as the reason in the audit trail.
  • Reject leaves the entry as it is. Type the reason in Remarks first: the user who asked reads it.
  • Withdraw the request (the user who asked, while it waits) takes the change back.

Good to know

  • A change is approved by someone other than the user who asked for it, with the Approve right on Make Journal Entries (System Administration > Page Access).
  • "The journal entry was changed since this request was made." The entry changed after the request: reject it and ask again on the entry as it is now.
  • A newer change to the same entry replaces the one waiting (it is marked Replaced by a later request).
  • Every request, approval, rejection and withdrawal is in the audit trail.

Financial reports

The reports of this module are listed under Financial > Reports in the sidebar, and in the Financial Reports card of the Report Center. They all read the general ledger, so they always agree with each other and with the transactions.

Every report has the common report tools: Refresh, Print / PDF, Export to spreadsheet, Email link, Modify Report and Memorize, and a click on an account or a number opens the detail behind it. These are explained once in Working with reports. This section describes what is special to each report.

Most financial reports have a Class filter. All shows everything; a class shows only the ledger lines of that class; (Unclassified) shows only the lines that have no class.

Trial Balance

The Trial Balance lists every account with a balance on a date, in a Debit or a Credit column, and checks that the two totals agree. Use it at month-end before you prepare the statements.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Trial Balance

Filters: As of (the date of the balances) and Class.

The columns: Account (code and name; click it to open its General Ledger), Type, Debit and Credit. The TOTAL row adds each column.

How to read it: balance sheet accounts show their balance at the date. Income and expense accounts show only the activity of the current fiscal year, from the first day of the fiscal year to the As of date, as the note above the table says. The net income of earlier years is included in Retained Earnings. If the two totals differ, the note ends with Out of balance! in red.

Tip

Accounts with a zero balance are left out, so the list stays short.

Trial Balance by Class

The Trial Balance with each class side by side: for every account, the debit or credit balance of each class, then the Total Debit and Total Credit of the whole company. Use it to see how each branch or division contributes to each account.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Trial Balance by Class, or the Trial Balance by Class button on the Class List. Also in the Financial Reports card of the Report Center.

Filters: As of and One column per class (debit less credit).

The columns: Account (click it to open its General Ledger), then for each class a Debit and a Credit column (the active classes and any class with activity, then Unclassified for entries without a class), then Total Debit and Total Credit. Click an amount to open the General Ledger of that account for that class. The TOTAL row adds each column. With One column per class (debit less credit) ticked, each class has a single column: debits as positive figures, credits as negative ones.

How to read it: as on the Trial Balance, balance sheet accounts show their balance at the date and income and expense accounts the fiscal year to date; the net income of earlier years is in Retained Earnings, class by class. The Total columns are the Trial Balance of the whole company and always agree with it. A class's own debits and credits can differ when an entry carries a different class on each side (a journal entry or cash voucher with a class on each line, or a payment applied across classes); the note above the table then names those classes.

Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet shows what the company owns, owes and is worth on a date.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Balance Sheet

Filters: As of and Class.

How to read it: the report has three parts. ASSETS has Current Assets (the Bank, Accounts Receivable and Other Current Asset types and the types of yours that behave as them), Fixed Assets and Other Assets. LIABILITIES has Current Liabilities (Accounts Payable, Credit Card and Other Current Liability) and Long-term Liabilities. EQUITY lists the equity accounts and the line Net Income (fiscal year to date); Retained Earnings includes the net income of earlier years. Each group has its total, and the last line is TOTAL LIABILITIES AND EQUITY.

Inside a group, every account type is a classification of its own: a heading with the name of the type, its accounts, and Total with the type's name (see Account Types). Where a group holds only one built-in type and none of yours, as Fixed Assets usually does, the type heading is left out. A sub-account is shown under its main account, and a main account with sub-accounts is a heading followed by its sub-accounts, a line (own entries) when something was posted to the main account itself, and its total. In the picture, Current Assets starts with the classification Cash: 1000 - Cash with its sub-accounts 1001 to 1020 and Total 1000 - Cash of 1,469,480.61, then 1009 - Cash on Hand - Accounting Office with 290,850.32, and Total Cash of 1,760,330.93.

Contra accounts show as negative figures: 1525 Accumulated Depreciation - Machinery shows -28,950.00 under Fixed Assets. The note above the table says "Assets = Liabilities + Equity." when the two sides agree.

Tip

For a short balance sheet with the main accounts only, open the Balance Sheet Summary.

Note

Filtered by class, the two sides may not agree, because a journal entry or cash voucher can carry a different class on each line. The note then explains this instead of showing an error.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Balance Sheet Summary

How to read it: the parts, groups and account-type classifications are those of the Balance Sheet, and so are all the totals. In the picture, 1000 - Cash is a single line of 1,469,480.61, the total of its eight sub-accounts, and Total Cash is 1,760,330.93 as on the Balance Sheet. Statement of Cash Flows

The Statement of Cash Flows explains how the cash went up or down in a period, by the indirect method.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Statement of Cash Flows

Filters: Dates (starts at Fiscal year to date), From, To and Class.

How to read it:

  • OPERATING ACTIVITIES starts from the Net Income of the period. The change in the accumulated depreciation accounts (Fixed Asset accounts with a Credit normal balance) is added back as "(depreciation)", because depreciation uses no cash. Then come the account types linked to operating activities, each with its accounts and the line Net change in and the type's name: with the built-in links, Accounts Receivable, Other Current Asset, Accounts Payable, Credit Card and Other Current Liability.
  • INVESTING ACTIVITIES shows the change in the accounts of the types linked to investing activities: with the built-in links, Fixed Asset and Other Asset.
  • FINANCING ACTIVITIES shows the change in the accounts of the types linked to financing activities: with the built-in links, Long Term Liability and Equity.
  • NET CASH INCREASE (DECREASE) FOR THE PERIOD, Cash at beginning of period and CASH AT END OF PERIOD close the report.

Which section an account is in follows its account type, as set under Account Types; a sub-account follows the type of its main account. Each account is shown once, as one line with the total of its sub-accounts for a main account, so the sections add up to the change in the cash. In the demo company the type Loans Receivable is an operating activity, shown with its own heading and Net change in Loans Receivable.

An increase in an asset uses cash and shows as a negative figure; an increase in a liability or equity provides cash and shows as a positive figure. The cash is the accounts of the types linked to Cash and cash equivalents, which the note above the table names, for example "Indirect method. Cash = the accounts of the types counted as cash and cash equivalents (Cash, Bank)". Money still in Undeposited Funds or in the card and e-wallet clearing accounts is treated as an operating asset until it is deposited, unless you give those accounts a type linked to cash and cash equivalents.

The cash at the end of the period equals the total of the cash accounts on the Balance Sheet at the same date: in the demo company, its Total Cash. If it does not, the note says "Does not tie to the cash accounts" in red with the balance it expected.

General Ledger

The General Ledger lists every entry posted to each account, with a running balance. It is where you land when you click an account on any other report.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > General Ledger, or click an account name on the Chart of Accounts or on a financial report.

Filters: Dates (starts at This month), From, To, Account (blank for all accounts), Class and Search (a word in the memo, the number or the name).

The columns: Date, Type, Num (click it to open the transaction), Name, Memo, Class, Debit, Credit and Balance.

How to read it: each account starts with its Beginning balance at the From date and ends with a Total line showing the total debits, total credits and the ending balance. The balance is shown on the account's normal side (its Normal balance on the Chart of Accounts): for an asset or expense account debits increase it and credits decrease it; for a liability, equity or income account, such as 4500 Water Sales or 2000 Accounts Payable, credits increase it and debits decrease it, so its credit balance shows as a positive amount. A negative balance means the account is on its other side. For income and expense accounts, the beginning balance counts only from the start of the fiscal year. Accounts with no entries and no beginning balance are left out.

Subsidiary Ledger

The Subsidiary Ledger lists the entries of one name (or of every name) on the accounts that keep a subsidiary ledger: a customer, a supplier, an employee or an other name, with a running balance per account.

Where to find it: Financial > Reports > Subsidiary Ledger, or click a name on the Schedule of Accounts.

Filters:

  • Dates, From and To (starts at This fiscal year).
  • Account: one account, or blank for every account marked With SL. While no account is marked With SL, a blank Account lists every account the names transacted.
  • Class.
  • Subsidiary ledger (name): one customer, supplier, employee or other name, or — every name —.
  • Which entries: From and to: the entries between the two dates, or To: every entry up to the To date.

The columns: Date, Ref ID (the transaction number; click it to open the transaction), Account, Description (the memo, or the transaction type), Class, Debit, Credit and Balance.

How to read it: the report is grouped by name, then by account, with a total line for each account and a Total row for the whole report. The Balance is debits less credits, running down each account. With From and to, the balance starts at zero on the From date, so it shows the movement of the period; choose To: every entry up to the To date to see what the name stands at on the To date. When you choose one name and leave Account blank, every account marked With SL is listed for that name, including those it has no entries on, so you see its whole subsidiary ledger at once.

Example

To see what an employee still owes on cash advances, choose the advances account in Account, the employee in Subsidiary ledger (name) and To: every entry up to the To date.
