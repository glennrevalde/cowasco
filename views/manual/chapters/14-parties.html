A multipurpose cooperative deals with the same people in several ways. A member saves and borrows, buys at the store on credit, delivers produce, and may also work for the cooperative. This chapter explains how the program keeps one record for each person or organization, the party, with every role it holds, and how the lines of business of the cooperative (savings and credit, the consumer store, marketing and others) are kept apart in the one general ledger.

The Parties menu holds the Party Center, Identity Review, the Consolidated Party Statement and three reports. Lines of Business and the Statement of Operations by Line of Business are in the Cooperative menu. The chapter also covers the party choice on the member, customer, supplier and other-name forms, the names printed on documents, the lock on posted documents, and the line of business on forms and reports.

How parties work

Each person or organization the cooperative deals with has one party record in the Party Master, with a Party ID that never changes: P-000001, P-000002 and so on. The party holds the legal name printed on documents, the name shown in lists, the TIN, the contact details, the address and the home branch.

What the party does for the cooperative is a role. Each role keeps its own record, with its own number, terms, limits, status and history, and that record points at its party:

Role The role record Number shown on the Party Center
MemberThe member on the Member CenterThe member no., for example 100001
CustomerThe customer on the Customer CenterC- and the record number, for example C-10
SupplierThe vendor on the Vendor CenterV- and the record number, for example V-9
EmployeeAn employee on Employees & Other NamesThe employee no. when one is entered, otherwise N- and the record number, for example N-18
Other nameAnother name (a payee) on Employees & Other NamesN- and the record number

A customer's jobs belong to the customer's party and are not roles of their own. A member's own ledger name on Employees & Other Names belongs to the member's party and is not listed as a separate role.

The rules the program keeps

  • Nothing is joined by name alone. A new member, customer, supplier or other name gets a new party unless you choose an existing one. When its details look like a party already on file, the program warns you before saving (see The party on member, customer, supplier and other-name forms). Possible duplicates already on file are listed on Identity Review, where a person decides.
  • Nothing is deleted. Giving, taking away, restoring or moving a role never deletes the role record or its documents. Taking a role away makes its record inactive; its number, open balance, documents and history stay.
  • Old numbers keep pointing to the party. The program keeps a crosswalk of every member, customer, supplier and other-name record and the party it belongs to. When two parties are linked, the party that is merged keeps its Party ID, which then points to the party kept.
  • Role history. Every role given, taken away, restored, linked or moved, every link of two parties and every change of the party's name is written to the party's role history, with the date, the user and the reason, and to the audit trail. The Party Role History report lists it.
  • One membership. A person or organization holds one membership. Membership starts on Member Information and ends or is restored through the member's status (resignation, termination, reinstatement), not on the Party Center. A change of membership status is also written to the role history.

Which record leads the details

The party and its role records share details. Which one leads depends on the roles the party holds:

  • A member leads its party. Saving the member on Member Information writes its details to the party: the kind of party, the legal name, the name in lists, the last, first and middle name and suffix, the date of birth, TIN, e-mail, mobile, address and home branch. A blank on the member does not erase a TIN, e-mail, mobile, address, date of birth or branch the party already has. On the Party Center these details of a member's party are read-only.
  • A party that is not a member and has one role follows that role record. When you change the customer, supplier or other name on its own page, the changes you make are written to the party.
  • A party with several roles is edited on the Party Center. The role records then only fill in details the party does not have yet: TIN, e-mail, mobile, phone, address, date of birth and home branch.

For a member who is a person, the legal name is the first name, the middle initial, the last name and the suffix, for example "Maria R. Santos Jr.", while the lists keep the member's name as "Santos, Maria R.". For an institution or a joint account, the legal name is the member's name.

Editing a party does not rename its customer, supplier or other-name records: each keeps the name it has in its own list. Documents print the party's legal name (see Names on documents, posted documents and reissues).

Kinds of party

A party is a Person or an Organization. An individual member or a joint account belongs to a person's party, and an institutional member to an organization's. The program refuses to attach a member to a party of the other kind, for example: "ABC Trading Corp. is an organization, and this member is an individual. Choose the party of the same kind, or none." Customers, suppliers and other names may belong to either kind.

A party's status is Active, Inactive (no longer dealt with, set with Edit party on the Party Center) or Merged (linked into another party on Identity Review). See Making a party inactive.

At the upgrade to this version

  • Every existing member, customer, supplier, employee and other name got a party of its own, numbered from P-000001. Nothing was merged, not even records with the same name. A customer's jobs took the customer's party, and a member's ledger name the member's party.
  • Institutional members became organizations and the other members persons. A customer or supplier with a company name became an organization, with the company name as its legal name; the others became persons.
  • The crosswalk records each record and the party it was given. The role history shows each role as "Granted" with the reason "Held at the upgrade".
  • Every existing document was given its party and the name, address and TIN to print, taken from its record at the upgrade and marked as coming from the upgrade.
  • Two lines of business were made, SC Savings and Credit and TR Consumer Store. The existing cooperative vouchers were given SC, and the sales, purchasing, POS and inventory documents TR; the other documents were left without a line of business (see Lines of Business).

Because nothing was merged, a member who was also a store customer now has two parties. After the upgrade, click Look for duplicates now on Identity Review and link the pairs that are the same person or organization.

Party Center

The Party Center lists every person and organization on file and shows everything about one of them: the details, what it owes and is owed, its roles, its documents and its role history. Here you create a party, give it a role, take a role away or restore it, and move a role that was attached to the wrong party.

Where to find it: Parties > Party Center. The Party ID shown on a customer, supplier or other-name record, on Member Information and on a saved document also opens it.

Finding a party

The list on the left shows each party's name in lists, its Party ID and its roles. "(inactive)" follows a role whose record is inactive, and "No role" marks a party without roles. A party that is not active shows its status after the roles, for example "Inactive".

  • Type in the search box (Name, party no., member no., TIN, mobile) to narrow the list as you type. Press Enter to search the whole list: the search looks at the Party ID, the legal name and the name in lists, the TIN, mobile and e-mail, the member no. and member name, and the customer and supplier names.
  • Every role narrows the list to one role (Member, Customer, Supplier, Employee or Other name), to More than one role or to No role.
  • The status choice shows the Active parties at first. Choose Inactive, Merged or All to see the others.
  • Every branch, shown when the cooperative has branches, narrows the list to the parties whose home branch, or whose member's branch, is the branch chosen.

The line under the filters, for example "34 of 34 parties", counts the parties shown. The list shows 100 parties at a time, with Previous and Next. Click a party to open it on the right.

The selected party

The top of the right side shows the name in lists, the Party ID and the kind of party, for example "P-000001, person", and the line "Printed on documents as Maria Santos, with member no. 100001". Under it are the details on file: TIN, Date of birth, Registration no., Mobile, Phone, E-mail, Address and Home branch. Only the details that are filled in are shown.

Three tiles give the party's position over all dates up to today, taken from the Consolidated Party Statement:

  • Party owes the cooperative: its accounts receivable and loans.
  • Cooperative owes the party: its accounts payable, savings deposits, time deposits, and interest on share capital and patronage refund still payable.
  • Member equity interest: its share capital. Shown for a member only.

The tiles are shown to users who may open the Consolidated Party Statement. In the screenshot, Maria Santos owes the cooperative ₱500.00, the cooperative owes her ₱4,130.31, and her share capital is ₱9,000.00.

The buttons under the tiles:

  • Edit party opens the party form (see New party and Edit party).
  • Consolidated statement opens the party's statement.
  • Enroll as member, for a party that is not a member, opens a new member on Member Information with the party chosen and its details filled in. It is shown to users who may add members.
  • Give a role makes a customer, supplier, employee or other-name record for the party.

For an inactive party the line under the name ends with ", inactive". Enroll as member and Give a role are shown for it too; giving it a role, or enrolling it as a member, makes it active again (see Making a party inactive).

A party that was linked into another shows "This party was merged into" and the party kept, with a link: its records and documents are there now. A merged party cannot be edited or given roles.

Making a party inactive

A party the cooperative no longer deals with can be made inactive: on Edit party, choose Inactive (no longer dealt with) in Status, type the reason and click Save party. This is allowed only when none of its roles is active. Take its customer, supplier, employee and other-name roles away first, and end a membership through the member's status; otherwise the program says, for example, "Party P-000035 still has an active role. Take its roles away first (a membership ends through its status on the Member Center)." Without a reason it says "Enter the reason for making the party inactive."

An inactive party is left out of the pick lists: Person or organization already on file, On file as, the Party of the Consolidated Party Statement, and the party lists of Move and Link two parties found another way. The one exception is Enroll as member, which opens On file as with the inactive party chosen. The party stays on the Party Center under Inactive or All, with its roles, documents and history. The possible-match warning and Identity Review still find it (see The possible-match warning).

The party becomes active again, with an entry in its role history, when:

  • its Status is set back to Active on Edit party, with a reason;
  • it is given a role with Give a role, or one of its roles is restored with Restore;
  • it is enrolled as a member with Enroll as member and the new member is saved;
  • one of its role records is saved as active on its own page, or its member is saved with an active, applicant or suspended membership;
  • it is the party kept on Identity Review and has an active role after the link.

Step by step

To create a party and give it its roles:

  1. Search the list first: the person or organization may already be on file.
  2. Click New party.
  3. Choose the Kind of party, then type the legal name (or, for a person, the names) and the details you have.
  4. Click Create party. If the details look like a party already on file, the program does not save and lists the possible parties. Open the existing party from the list instead, or, when it is really a different person or organization, tick This is a different person or organization and click Create party again.
  5. The new party opens with "Party created. Give it its roles below."
  6. Click Give a role, fill in the dialog (below) and click Give the role. The program names the record, for example "Supplier role given (V-12, Bright Minds Academy)." To make the party a member, click Enroll as member instead.

The Give a role dialog:

Field What to enter / what it means
RoleCustomer (buys from the cooperative), Supplier (sells or delivers to the cooperative), Employee, or Other name (payee).
Terms, Credit limit (0 = none), Price level, Can charge purchases at the POSFor a customer. Leave Price level at its blank choice, Member prices of the line of business (follows the membership) for a member's party or Nonmember prices of the line of business for others, and the customer gets no price level of its own: each sale then takes the member or nonmember prices of its line of business, according to the party's membership at the time (see Member and nonmember prices). Choose a level only for a customer who should always have it.
Employee no., Position / relationshipFor an employee or other name.
Reason or reference (recorded with the role)Optional, for example "Member buying on credit at the store". It goes to the role history.

The role record is made from the party: its name is the party's name in lists, an organization's legal name becomes its company name, and it takes the party's phone, mobile, e-mail, address and TIN. Its own number, terms and history start then. If the party had the same role before and it was taken away, the old record is restored instead, so its number and history continue. To give the customer role you also need the Create right on the Customer Center; for a supplier, on the Vendor Center; for an employee or other name, on Employees & Other Names.

To take a role away, or restore it:

  1. Click Take away (or Restore) on the role's row.
  2. Type the reason, which is required and goes to the role history and the audit trail.
  3. Click Confirm. The record becomes inactive (or active again); its number, open balance, documents and history stay. A customer's jobs follow the customer.

To move a role that was attached to the wrong party:

  1. Click Move on the role's row.
  2. In To party, choose the party the record belongs to, or leave A new party made from the record.
  3. Type the reason, for example what shows the record belongs to the other party.
  4. Click Move. The record and its documents go to the other party (a customer with its jobs); the names printed on the documents stay as issued. The program opens the party the role went to. The role history of both parties shows "Moved out" and "Moved in".

Confidential member details

For a party that is a member, users without the special permission "Cooperative: can see confidential member details (birth date, TIN, contact numbers, home address)" see the TIN, date of birth, e-mail, mobile, phone, address and registration no. with all but the last three characters replaced by asterisks (a value of four characters or fewer is all asterisks), and the line "Confidential member details are partly hidden." On Edit party those details are not shown at all: "The member's contact details and TIN are confidential and are not shown to you." The details of parties that are not members are shown in full. Identity Review, the Consolidated Party Statement and the Party List hide them the same way.

The same permission applies on Member Information. For an existing member, a user without it sees the date of birth, TIN, mobile, e-mail and the residence and provincial addresses partly hidden in locked boxes, and instead of the contact numbers the line "Hidden: you need the special permission to see confidential member details. Saving this page keeps the member's numbers as they are." When that user saves the member, these details stay as they were. The form of a new member is not hidden.

The Customer Center and the Vendor Center show the contact details, billing address and TIN of the customer or supplier record itself. They belong to the role record and are shown to anyone who may open those pages: the hiding applies to the details of the member and of its party, not to these records.

Good to know

  • "Enter the legal name (or the first and last name of a person)."
  • "The e-mail address is not valid." and "Enter a valid date of birth."
  • "Enter the reason for changing the legal name (new documents print it; documents already issued keep the name they were issued with)."
  • "Santos, Maria is already a customer (Maria Santos)." The party already has an active record of that role; open it from the Roles table. The same message is given for a supplier, and "is already on Employees & Other Names" for an employee or other name.
  • "You are not allowed to add customers." (or suppliers, or employees and other names): you have no Create right on the page of that role.
  • "You are not allowed to move roles between parties (special permission: link or move parties)."
  • "Party P-000035 still has an active role. Take its roles away first (a membership ends through its status on the Member Center)." and "Enter the reason for making the party inactive." (see Making a party inactive).
  • "The role is already on that party."
  • The member row has no Take away or Move: a membership is ended or restored through the member's status (resignation, termination, reinstatement), and a membership is never moved. If two parties are the same member, link them on Identity Review.

The party on member, customer, supplier and other-name forms

The forms that add a member, a customer, a supplier or an employee or other name ask whom the new record belongs to. A member who starts buying at the store, or a supplier who joins the cooperative, then stays one party with two roles.

Person or organization already on file

On a new customer (Customer Center), a new supplier (Vendor Center) and a new employee or other name (Employees & Other Names), the first field is Person or organization already on file. Type part of a name to search. Each active party is listed with its name in lists, Party ID and roles, for example "Santos, Maria (P-000001), Member, Customer, Supplier, Employee".

  • Choose the party when the person or organization is already on file. The new record becomes a role of that party, and details the party does not have yet are filled in from the record. The role history shows "Linked" with "Linked to an existing party".
  • Leave it at "Not on file: a new party made from this customer" (or supplier, or name) for someone new. A new party is made from the record. The role history shows "Granted" with "New record".

The field is on new records only. A job takes the party of the customer it belongs to, whatever is chosen.

The possible-match warning

When no party is chosen, the program compares the new record's TIN, e-mail, mobile and phone numbers and name with the active and inactive parties before saving. If any look the same, the record is not saved and the form shows up to four of them, the closest first, for example:

"This customer may already be on file: Cruz, Ana (P-000003, Member; same mobile number, same name). Choose the existing party, or tick "This is a different person or organization" to make a new one."

The tick box This is a different person or organization then appears next to the field.

An inactive party is marked ", inactive" after its Party ID, and the message adds after "Choose the existing party": "(an inactive party is made active again on the Party Center first, or by giving it the role there)". An inactive party is not in the pick list, so open it on the Party Center and either give it the role there with Give a role (for a new member, Enroll as member), or make it active with Edit party and then choose it on the form.

To answer the warning:

  1. Check the parties named in the message, on the Party Center if you need to.
  2. If one of them is the same person or organization, choose it in Person or organization already on file and save again.
  3. If none is, tick This is a different person or organization and save again. A new party is made.

The party line on saved records

On the edit form and the detail panel of a customer or supplier, and on the edit form of an employee or other name, a line names the party, for example: "Party P-000001, printed on documents as Maria Santos (member no. 100001). Also member, supplier, employee." The Party ID opens the Party Center for users who may open it. A saved record is not moved to another party from its own form; use Move on the Party Center.

Member Information

On a new member, the field above the tabs is On file as (a customer, supplier or other name joining the cooperative). It lists the active parties that are not members yet. Choose the party when the new member is already on file, for example a store customer joining the cooperative; otherwise leave it at Not on file: a new party. Enroll as member on the Party Center opens the form with the party chosen and fills in the names, TIN, e-mail, mobile, date of birth and branch from the party. The party is chosen even when it is inactive; it is then listed with ", inactive" after its Party ID, for example "(P-000012, inactive)", and saving the member makes it active again. An organization is filled in as an institutional member, with its legal name as the name of the institution.

When no party is chosen and the details look like a party that is not a member, the member is not saved and the form says, for example: "This person may already be on file: Ana M. Cruz (P-000035, Customer; same mobile number, same name). Choose the existing party, or tick "This is a different person" to make a new one. To use it, choose it under "On file as"." The tick box This is a different person appears next to Add member. Parties that are already members are left out of this check; the member check for the same name and date of birth covers them.

The chosen party must be of the same kind as the member (see Kinds of party). Once the member is saved, the member record leads the party's details.

On a saved member, the line next to Kind of member reads "Party P-000001: documents print the member's registered name." The Party ID opens the Party Center.

To enroll a store customer as a member:

  1. Open Parties > Party Center and find the customer's party.
  2. Click Enroll as member. Member Information opens with the party chosen in On file as and its details filled in.
  3. Complete the member's details and membership on the tabs, as for any new member.
  4. Click Add member. The member joins the party; the role history shows "Linked" with "Enrolled as a member". The customer record, its number and its documents stay as they were.

Identity Review

Identity Review lists pairs of parties that may be the same person or organization, and records what a person decides: the two are the same and are linked into one party, or they are different. The program never links parties on its own. As the page says: "Records are never joined by name alone."

Where to find it: Parties > Identity Review. The Identity Review button on the Party Center also opens it and shows the number of pairs waiting, for example "Identity Review (2)". Review and link on Identity Review under May be the same as on the Party Center records that party's possible pairs and opens Identity Review on them.

How possible pairs are found

Look for duplicates now compares every party that is not merged. Two parties become a pair when they share at least one of the following. The pair's score is the sum of what they share; the higher the score, the more likely they are the same.

Basis What is compared Score
Same TINThe first nine digits of the TIN, without dashes or spaces. A TIN of zeros does not count.60
Same name and date of birthThe name, as compared below, together with the date of birth.50
Same e-mailThe e-mail address, without regard to capitals.30
Same mobile number, Same phone numberThe last ten digits of the mobile and phone numbers, with spaces, dashes, brackets and other characters left out. A phone number and a mobile number match each other, so a mobile number typed in the phone box of a customer or supplier is found. Numbers shorter than ten digits are not compared.30
Same nameThe legal name or the name in lists, without capitals, punctuation, initials or word order: "Santos, Maria R." and "Maria Santos" are the same name.20

A value shared by more than 40 parties, such as a placeholder TIN or e-mail, is not used. Pairs already decided stay decided; pairs still to review get their basis and score brought up to date. The program says how many new pairs it found: "2 new possible duplicates found." or "No new possible duplicates."

The pairs

The tabs To review, Linked, Different and All show the pairs by decision, with their counts. The pairs to review come first, the highest score first.

Each pair's heading says what the two share, for example "Same mobile number, Same name", with "Score 50" on the right; a decided pair shows instead "Linked" or "Different", the date and time, the user and the reason. Each side shows the party's name (click it to open the Party Center), the Party ID and kind, the roles, and the Legal name, TIN, Date of birth, Mobile, Phone (when it differs from the mobile), E-mail and Address.

Under a pair to review, when neither party is merged, are the buttons Same: keep P-000003, Same: keep P-000035 (one for each party) and Different. This includes pairs with an inactive party. When the page was opened for one party, the line "Showing pairs with party" names it, and Show every pair shows them all again.

What linking does

  • Every role record of the other party (its membership, customers with their jobs, suppliers, employees and other names) and every one of its documents moves to the party kept.
  • Details the party kept does not have (TIN, e-mail, mobile, phone, address, date of birth, home branch) are filled in from the other party. When the membership came from the other party, the member's details then lead the party kept.
  • The other party stays, with the status Merged, pointing to the party kept. Its Party ID still opens it on the Party Center, with the link to the party kept.
  • When the party kept was inactive and now has an active role, it becomes active again.
  • The names already printed on documents stay as issued.
  • The role history of both parties shows "Merged" with the reason and who approved it, and the link is written to the audit trail.
  • Pairs still to review that involve the merged party are removed; the next Look for duplicates now finds them again against the party kept.

Two members are never linked: "Both parties are members (100003 and 100013). A person holds one membership: settle and close one membership first."

Step by step

To decide a pair:

  1. Open Parties > Identity Review. After adding or importing records, click Look for duplicates now.
  2. On To review, compare the two sides. Open a name to see the party's roles and documents on the Party Center.
  3. If they are different people or organizations, click Different, type the reason (what shows they are different) and click Record. The program says "Recorded as two different parties." The pair moves to Different and is not proposed again.
  4. If they are the same, click Same: keep on the party to keep, usually the member. In Link the parties, check the line that says which party is kept and which is moved into it, and type the reason (what shows they are the same).
  5. If you do not have the Approve right on the page, open Supervisor approval and have a supervisor type their username and password, or type a one-time approval code.
  6. Click Link. The program says "The parties were linked. The party kept has every role and document now." and opens the party kept on the Party Center.

To link two parties that were not proposed: under Link two parties found another way, choose the Party to keep and the Party to move into it (both lists hold the active parties), type the Reason (what shows they are the same), have a supervisor approve if needed, and click Link the parties.

Example

Ana M. Cruz (P-000035), a store customer, has the same mobile number as the member Cruz, Ana (P-000003). The membership officer confirms that they are the same person and clicks Same: keep P-000003 with the reason "Same person, mobile number and address confirmed by phone on 10/12/2026". The customer record and its invoices move to P-000003, and P-000035 stays as a merged party pointing to it.

Who may do what

Names on documents, posted documents and reissues

The name printed on a document

When a document is saved, the program takes what it will print about the counterparty from the party master at that moment, and keeps it on the document:

  • Name: the party's legal name.
  • Address: the address typed on the document, otherwise the address of the role record (the customer's billing address, or the customer's for a job; the supplier's or the other name's address), otherwise the party's address.
  • TIN: the TIN of the role record (or of the customer, for a job), otherwise the party's.
  • Member no.: the member no. of the party's membership, when it has one.

These are taken again only when the customer, supplier, name or payee on the document is changed; any other edit keeps them. Renaming a member, customer or supplier later therefore does not change the documents already issued. A document made out to a name that is on no list prints that name. Documents saved before the upgrade print what was taken at the upgrade.

The document's page shows what it prints as, for example, Bill to (printed): the name, "Member no. 100001", "Reissued 1 time" after a correction, and a link to the party, such as "Party P-000001". It also shows the Line of business.

Labels and the member number

The label in front of the name follows the kind of document:

With Print the member no. under the name of a member on invoices, receipts, checks and vouchers ticked on Lines of Business, the document of a member shows the member no. with the name, for example "Member no. 100001" under the name of an invoice, or "(member no. 100001)" after the payee on a check or voucher. Untick it and the number is not shown.

Posted documents are locked

With Lock posted documents ticked on Lines of Business (the upgrade turns it on), the amounts, accounts, names, dates and lines of a posted document can be changed only by a user with the special permission "Can change or delete a posted document while posted documents are locked (with a reason)", and only with a reason. Nobody else can delete a posted document: they void it, or record a correcting document such as a credit memo, a supplier debit memo or a journal entry.

  • A document is posted when it has general ledger entries and is not void, pending or suspended. Estimates, sales orders and purchase orders do not post and are not locked.
  • The memo, the reference and similar details can still be changed by anyone with the Edit right.
  • The page of a locked document shows "Posted. Changes to amounts, accounts, names, dates or lines need a reason and are kept in the audit trail." to users with the permission, and "Posted. Only the memo, reference and similar details can be changed; correct amounts with a void or a correcting document." to the others. Delete is not offered to users without the permission.
  • On the form of a locked document, users with the permission get the box Reason for changing this posted document: "Needed when amounts, accounts, names, dates or lines change. The document as it was stays in the audit trail."

Messages:

  • "This document is posted, and posted documents are locked: amounts, accounts, names, dates and lines cannot be changed. Void it and record it again, record a correcting document (credit memo, vendor credit, journal entry), or ask a user who may change posted documents."
  • "Enter the reason for changing this posted document (it is kept in the audit trail with the document as it was)."
  • "Posted documents are locked and cannot be deleted. Void it instead: the document stays, marked void, with the reason."

Cooperative vouchers (official receipts, member disbursements, loan releases and cooperative journal vouchers) are never edited or deleted in any case; they are voided.

Correcting the printed name

When a document was issued with a wrong name, address or TIN, for example with the middle initial missing, Correct printed name on the document's page corrects only what is printed. The amounts, the accounts and the ledger do not change, and the document keeps its number. The correction is a reissue: it is numbered (Reissue no. 1, 2 and so on), kept with the old and new name, address and TIN, the reason and who approved it, listed in Reissued Documents and written to the audit trail. The print-out then shows "Reissue no. 1 of this document" with the date.

The button is shown to users with the special permission "Can correct the name, address or TIN printed on an issued document (a reissue, with a reason)" and the Print right on the document's page. It is not offered on a voided document or on a document with no printed name.

Each correction is approved. A user with the Approve right on the document's page approves their own correction. Anyone else needs a supervisor who has that right, who types their username and password under Supervisor approval in the dialog, or a one-time approval code from Approval Codes. The dialog says so: "A user without the Approve right on this page needs a supervisor's approval." The approver is shown in the Approved by column of Reissued Documents.

To correct the name printed on a document:

  1. If the name is wrong at its source, correct it there first: on Member Information for a member, otherwise with Edit party on the Party Center (or on the role record, for a party with one role).
  2. Open the document and click Correct printed name.
  3. The boxes Name to print, Address and TIN hold the party master's current details; the line under them gives the name printed now. Change them if needed. An address or TIN box left empty takes the party master's current detail.
  4. Type the reason, which is required and goes to the audit trail.
  5. If you do not have the Approve right on the document's page, open Supervisor approval and have a supervisor type their username and password, or type a one-time approval code.
  6. Click Correct printed name. The program says, for example, "Invoice INV-00015 reissued with the corrected name. The correction is kept in the audit trail."
Example

Invoice INV-00015 of ₱500.00 was issued to Maria Santos. Her legal name is corrected to Maria R. Santos, then the invoice is reissued with the reason "Middle initial missing on the issued invoice". The invoice still owes ₱500.00 and posts the same entries; it now prints "Maria R. Santos" and "Reissue no. 1 of this document, 10/12/2026".

When the correction is refused, the document's page shows the message and the dialog opens again by itself with the name, address, TIN and reason as you typed them; when a supervisor's approval is needed, Supervisor approval is open. Correct what the message asks and click Correct printed name again. The messages:

  • "The name, address and TIN are the same as on the document."
  • "Enter the reason for the correction."
  • "Supervisor approval is required: correction of the name printed on Invoice INV-00015. Ask a supervisor to enter their username and password below, or type a one-time approval code."
  • "Approval failed: the supervisor username or password is wrong."
  • "You are not allowed to correct the name printed on an issued document (special permission: reissue documents)."

Lines of Business

A multipurpose cooperative runs several lines of business: savings and credit, a consumer store, wholesale or member purchasing, marketing of members' produce, production, services. Each document carries one line of business, and so do its ledger lines, so the one general ledger can be reported for the whole cooperative or for one line at a time. A line sets who may transact in it, the price levels of members and nonmembers, and whether its member transactions count for patronage refund.

The same page holds the default line of each kind of document, the lines of the program the cooperative runs, the member and nonmember prices, the document rules (the lock on posted documents and the member no. on documents), the goods and services that count for patronage refund, and the inter-unit accounts.

Where to find it: Members & Others > Lines of Business.

The lines of business

The list shows each line with its Code, Line of business (the name and kind), Who may transact, Patronage (Counts or Does not count), Prices (member / nonmember) (the price levels that apply in the line: its own, otherwise the level set under Prices when the line of business has none followed by "(general)", otherwise "Standard") and the number of Documents. An inactive line is greyed. Edit opens a line in the form on the right.

Member and nonmember prices

When a sale is entered, the price of an item comes from the first price level found in this order:

  1. The customer's own price level, on the customer record. "A customer's own price level always comes first."
  2. The line of business's price level of members, when the customer's party is an active member (an applicant or a suspended member counts as a member), or its price level of nonmembers otherwise. A walk-in sale with no customer uses the nonmember level.
  3. The level set under Prices when the line of business has none, for members or for nonmembers and walk-in customers.
  4. Otherwise, the standard price of the item.

A price level can have dates. On Terms, Tax Codes & Other Lists, the Price levels list has Valid from and Valid until: a level with dates applies only to documents dated within them, for example a promotion for one month; a level without dates always applies. Outside its dates, or when it is inactive, the level is not used and the standard price applies.

Documents without a line of business

This box is shown to users with the Edit right while some documents have no line of business. It counts them by kind, for example "General journal, depreciation, asset disposals and opening balances: 3".

To give older documents a line of business:

  1. In Documents, choose the kind of documents.
  2. In Give them, choose the line of business.
  3. Type the Reason.
  4. Click Assign. Every document of that kind without a line of business gets it, for example "3 document(s) given SC Savings and Credit." "Only the line of business is recorded on the documents and their ledger lines; amounts, numbers and dates do not change." The assignment is written to the audit trail.

Goods and services that count for patronage refund

"Not every member sale qualifies: only these items, in lines of business that count." The goods and services are the items of the types Inventory part, Non-inventory part, Inventory assembly and Service. The box says how many of the active ones count, for example "19 of 19 active goods and services count."

To mark many items at once, choose a Category (or Every good and service), choose Count or Do not count in They, and click Apply. Only the goods and services of the category are changed; other charges, discounts and the other item types are left as they are. The program says how many items were updated. One item at a time, tick Counts for patronage refund on the item in Item List. New items do not count until they are marked.

Inter-unit accounts

When one line of business lends to, sells to or charges another, the books use accounts kept only for these dealings: a due-to and due-from account between the lines, and, for sales and charges between them, an inter-unit sales account and the matching cost account. In Due to / due from, inter-unit sales and charges, and their cost, tick these accounts among the asset, liability, income and expense accounts, and click Save. The program says "Inter-unit accounts saved."

"Accounts used only for dealings between lines of business, such as Due from Consumer Store and Due to Savings and Credit, or Inter-unit Sales and Inter-unit Purchases. They show on each unit's statements and are eliminated (left out) when the whole cooperative is reported: what the units owe each other nets out, and what they sold or charged each other nets out against its cost, so nothing is counted twice."

Elimination means that, on the statements of the whole cooperative, the inter-unit accounts are left out, because what one line owes, sells or charges another is not a dealing with anyone outside the cooperative. The asset and liability accounts are netted together, and the income and expense accounts together, each group on its own. See Eliminations.

The Line of business field on documents

While two or more lines are active, the document forms of Customers and Vendors (estimates, orders, invoices, sales receipts, payments, credit memos, purchase orders, item receipts, bills, bill payments, advances), and Write Checks, Make Deposits, Cash Voucher and Make Journal Entries show a Line of business field. It starts with the default line for that kind of document; a saved document shows its own line. Leave it blank and the document has no line of business. Documents entered without the field, such as cooperative vouchers, POS sales and inventory documents, take the default of their kind.

The forms with an inventory site follow the usual site of the line of business. A new sales order, invoice, sales receipt, credit memo, purchase order, bill, supplier debit memo or check, and a new receipt on Receive Items (when it does not come from a purchase order), starts at the usual site of its default line of business, or at the first site when the line has none. Choosing on the form a line of business that has a usual site moves the form's site to it; you can still change the site afterwards.

When a sale (an invoice, sales receipt, sales order, statement charge or POS sale) is saved for the first time, and again when an edit changes its customer or its line of business, the program checks who may transact in its line of business. The customer counts as a member when its party has an active membership; an applicant or a suspended member counts too. In a marketing line, the same check applies to the supplier of a purchase order, item receipt or bill, when it is first saved and when an edit changes the supplier or the line of business.

  • Members only: a document made out to a nonmember or a walk-in customer is refused, for example "Savings and Credit serves members only, and this invoice is made out to a nonmember. Choose a member, or record it under another line of business."
  • Nonmembers need approval: a user with the Approve right on the document's page saves the document as usual; others need a supervisor's approval on the form, for example "Supervisor approval is required: buy from a nonmember in Marketing of Produce. Ask a supervisor to enter their username and password below, or type a one-time approval code." The supervisor is recorded as the approver of the document.
  • Members and nonmembers: nothing is checked.

  2. In New line of business, type the Code, for example MK, and the Name, for example Marketing of Produce.
  3. Choose the Kind, for example Marketing of members' produce, and Who may transact, for example Nonmembers need approval.
  4. Choose the price levels of members and nonmembers, if the line has its own.

Reporting by line of business

The Line of business filter

Every report with a Class filter gets a Line of business filter next to it while two or more lines of business are active, for example the Trial Balance, the Balance Sheet, the Income Statement and the General Ledger. The cooperative Statement of Financial Condition and Statement of Operations always have it.

Choose a line and the report counts only the ledger lines of that line's documents; the note above the report reads, for example, "Line of business: SC Savings and Credit only." Leave it at All for the whole cooperative.

Eliminations

When the whole cooperative is reported and inter-unit accounts are marked on Lines of Business, the financial statements eliminate the dealings between lines of business: the Trial Balance, the Balance Sheet, the Income Statement, the Statement of Cash Flows, and the cooperative Statement of Financial Condition and Statement of Operations. Their note reads "Whole cooperative; balances and charges between lines of business are eliminated."

  • The inter-unit asset and liability accounts are netted together: what the lines owe each other.
  • The inter-unit income and expense accounts are netted together: what the lines sold or charged each other, against its cost.
  • Only a difference that does not net to zero, such as an entry recorded on one side only, stays, on the first account of its group that has a balance, so the statements still balance and show it.

The General Ledger, the Journal, the Trial Balance by Date Range, the Trial Balance of Totals and the Balance Sheet by Date Range show every entry as it was booked, and their note reads "Whole cooperative; entries between lines of business are listed as booked (they are eliminated only on the financial statements)." Run for one line of business, every report shows the inter-unit accounts as they are.

The Statement of Operations by Line of Business shows each line side by side, with the elimination of the inter-unit income and expense accounts in a column of its own.

Accounts receivable and payable in the reconciliation

The Subsidiary vs General Ledger Reconciliation now also has the lines Accounts receivable (customers) and Accounts payable (suppliers). Each compares the open balances of the documents on the date (invoices, statement charges and finance charges less unapplied payments and credit memos; bills less supplier debit memos) with the accounts receivable or accounts payable control accounts. When they differ, the customers or suppliers whose open documents differ from their ledger balance are listed under the line, with their C- or V- number; click one to open it on the Customer Center or Vendor Center. "(no name on the ledger line)" collects entries made to a control account with no customer or supplier. A journal entry posted to a control account without a document shows as a difference.

The patronage refund basis

On a Patronage Refund batch, Patronage measured by chooses the basis of each member's share, between the dates in Patronage from and to:

Choice What is counted for each member
Income earned from the member (the income accounts chosen)The credits less the debits on the Income accounts (patronage) you choose, on ledger lines that carry the member's name or are made out to a customer record of the member's party. A line of an item counts only when the item counts for patronage refund.
Member's purchases of goods and services that countThe item lines of the invoices, sales receipts, POS sales and statement charges made out to a customer record of the member's party, less credit memos and POS returns, for the items that count. Voided, pending and suspended documents are left out.
Member's deliveries (produce sold to the cooperative) that countThe item lines of the bills, item receipts and checks from a supplier record of the member's party, less supplier debit memos, for the items that count. Voided and pending documents are left out.

With each basis, only documents in lines of business whose member transactions count (or with no line of business) are taken. "A member is recognized through the party, also when buying or selling as a customer or supplier." The rest of the batch (method, credit, approval, posting) is as described under Interest on Share Capital and Patronage Refund.

Expenses are shown as positive amounts; sales returns and discounts show as negative revenues. Click an account to open its General Ledger for the period.

The note above the table says how the lines are taken from the one general ledger. With inter-unit income or expense accounts marked it reads "Sales and charges between lines of business (the inter-unit income and expense accounts) net out in the Eliminations column; a difference that does not net shows on the whole-cooperative column."; without them, "No inter-unit income or expense accounts are set, so nothing is eliminated (set them on Members & Others > Lines of Business)." When some documents have no line of business, it adds "Documents without a line of business are shown apart; give them one on Members & Others > Lines of Business."

Tip

In the sample cooperative, the depreciation expense of ₱113,950.00 falls under No line of business because the default line for the general journal and depreciation is None. Assign those documents on Documents without a line of business, or set a default for them, so that each line carries its own costs.

The Approve right is used on these pages:

  • Identity Review: linking two parties needs the Approve right on the page, or a supervisor who has it approves on the form.
  • Correct printed name: a correction is approved by the user's own Approve right on the document's page, or by a supervisor who has it.
  • Lines of Business: turning Lock posted documents on or off needs the Approve right on the page. There is no supervisor approval for this.
  • Documents in a line of business with "Nonmembers need approval": a sale to a nonmember (or, in a marketing line, a purchase from a nonmember), when it is first saved or when an edit changes the customer, supplier or line of business, needs the Approve right on the document's page, or a supervisor who has it approves on the form.
