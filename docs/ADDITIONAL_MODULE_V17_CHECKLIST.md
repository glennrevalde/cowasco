## 2. Decisions

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | The editor lives on Billing Adjustments itself (`?edit=<type>-<id>`), not on a new page. | The same rights (the page and a Super Administrator) and the same menu entry; nothing new to grant in Page Access. |
| D2 | The fields that can be corrected are the ones the controlled correction already corrected (reading, quantity or amount, due date); the charge category and the date issued of a one-time bill stay on One-Time Billing (Update Bill Record), as the bill's footer says. | A different charge or date issues the bill again with other accounts; One-Time Billing already does that with its own rights. Billing Adjustments keeps the super-administrator correction of the amounts. |
| D3 | The preview is worked out on the server by the functions the save uses (`bl_water_correction`, `bl_other_correction`), never in the browser. | What is shown is what is saved: the tariff, discounts, centavo splits and dates have one implementation. |
| D4 | A water bill is priced at the current tariff and discounts when it is corrected (as before); the editor says so when that differs from the bill as it stands. | The correction re-snapshots the bill; the operator sees the amount before saving. |
| D5 | The Individual Statement lists the invoices by their balance now; the statement date decides which invoices (dated on or before it) and what is overdue. | It answers "what is unpaid", whatever the billing period; the statement of one billing period stays for the period view. |
| D5b | A member or customer is a party: the statement covers all of its water-service accounts, or one of them. | The request names the member or customer; a member with two houses has two accounts and one statement. |
| D6 | Fixed Share Capital bills are offered for the account's member (the same party), as the cashier collects them; a share capital bill not yet posted is not (nothing is posted by a statement). | The statement shows what the cashier will collect; reading must not post. |
| D7 | Penalties are not added on the statement. | The penalty is worked out when the bill is paid (the cashier), as on the statement of one billing period. |

## 3. Independent review

A separate reviewer read the whole change against the requests (security, money and dates, regressions, MySQL 5.5,
JavaScript, the pages on a phone), ran `php -l` and `node --check`, and ran the new functions on the sample database in
a read-only transaction. No security problem: every new endpoint checks the rights (and the bill editor's a Super
Administrator and the form's token), all output is escaped, all SQL is bound, another account's invoices are refused.
Fixed after it:

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Saving any correction of a recurring bill changed its term (the due date, sent unchanged, was counted from the bill date). | A due date left as it is keeps the due date, term and disconnection date; a new one counts the term of a recurring bill from the end of its service period. |
| 2 | Installment amortizations not yet due were ticked at first and added to the total due. | Listed, not ticked at first (the page says how many); ticked by hand they are shown as not yet due. |
| 3 | A member's share capital bills were on each of his water accounts' statements; the member was listed once per account. | One name per member or customer with all its accounts (or one); a share capital bill on the first account only. |
| 4 | The preview of a water bill provisioned the Water Bill code (row lock, settings written) on every keystroke. | The tariff helpers take a read-only switch; the preview reads only (a harness check runs it in a read-only transaction). |
| 5 | A late statement answer could show under the next member chosen. | A sequence and the member are checked before it is shown. |
| 6 | "2" and "2.00" counted as a change (an identical -R entry could be posted). | Numbers compared as numbers. |
| 7 | The term and disconnection date shown did not always follow; a bill with no due date showed "0 days". | They follow the due date and return to the bill's own; "—" without a due date. |
| 8 | Ctrl+P on the Individual tab printed the other statement. | The tab prints its own statement. |
| 9 | The name search counted the unpaid invoices as of today. | As of the statement date. |
| 10 | A water bill with no due date was shown overdue from its period end. | Its own due date, as at the cashier (none: "No due date"). |
| 11 | Another account's invoice left off was called "no longer unpaid". | Worded for both cases. |
| 13 | The editor showed the bill priced again at today's set-up as soon as it opened. | The bill stays as issued until something is changed; a different price at today's set-up is said beside it. |
| 14 | The amount box of a discounted bill sat on the line, holding the net total. | On the Total amount line, the discount kept. |
| 15 | A bill whose due date was before its bill date could not be corrected at all. | An unchanged due date is not checked again. |

Left as they are, by design: the tab's underline uses the program's primary blue (#0d6efd, the colour theme.css gives the
primary buttons) (12); the statement date chooses the invoices and the overdue days, the balances are those of today
(16); the charge category and date issued of a one-time bill stay on One-Time Billing, and statements are one member or
customer at a time (17).
